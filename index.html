How to prepare

  1. Look back through your exercise book at the tasks and questions from your lessons.
  2. Missed a lesson? Ask your teacher, or someone in your class, what you missed and catch up.
  3. Read the Revision tab and do the quiz at the end of each part.
  4. Use BBC Bitesize KS3 History and Oak National Academy KS3 History.

To get the very top marks

  1. Answer every multiple choice question. Never leave one blank: take your best guess.
  2. Be ready for WHY and HOW questions, not just dates. Don’t just remember that Charles I was executed in 1649: understand why it was so extraordinary, and how it changed England.
  3. Learn the word “astonishing”. It means very surprising. Be ready to explain why Nur Jahan’s actions were unusual for a woman of her time.
  4. Section C essay: use PEEL. Point: say what changed or stayed the same. Evidence: give a fact or date. Explain: why it matters. Link back to the question. Then write a paragraph that starts However, and finish with a judgement.

Part 1

Who was Nur Jahan?

Mughal Empire, India · 1577 to 1645

Who was she?

  • A powerful woman in the Mughal Empire in India.
  • Born in 1577. Her birth name was Mehr-un-Nissa.
  • She married Emperor Jahangir in 1611.
  • Her name Nur Jahan means Light of the World.
  • The Mughal Empire was wealthy and powerful, and ruled much of the Indian subcontinent.

Why was she important?

  • Women in Mughal royal society were expected to stay away from public political leadership. However, Nur Jahan:
  • Influenced important political decisions and government.
  • Had coins made with her name alongside Jahangir’s.
  • Issued royal orders and had influence over powerful nobles.
  • Took part in hunting and other activities normally done by male rulers.
Remember: Nur Jahan was astonishing because she gained an extraordinary amount of political power in a society where women were not usually expected to rule.

Nur Jahan’s political power

What was astonishing about it?

Four signs of her political power

1

Influenced government

  • Advised Emperor Jahangir.
  • Helped make important decisions.
  • Powerful nobles had to consider her.
2

Coins in her name

  • Coins were made with her name alongside Jahangir’s.
  • Coins were a symbol of royal authority.
3

Issued royal orders

  • She could give orders and make decisions.
  • Her authority went beyond advising her husband.
4

Royal appointments

  • She helped decide who got important government jobs.
  • So she affected who held power.

Part 3

Nur Jahan’s shikar

Shikar means hunting

What was shikar?

  • Shikar means hunting.
  • For Mughal emperors and powerful men it was not just entertainment. It showed:
  • Courage, physical strength and leadership.
  • Military skills.
  • Power and authority.

What did Nur Jahan do?

  • She was famous for taking part in hunting expeditions.
  • She hunted dangerous animals, including tigers.
  • Historical accounts describe her as an exceptionally skilled huntress.

Why was this astonishing?

  • Hunting dangerous animals was normally done by men.
  • It showed physical courage and strength.
  • It was linked to emperors and powerful male nobles.
  • It strengthened her image as a powerful ruler.
Exam sentence to remember“Nur Jahan’s shikar was astonishing because hunting dangerous animals was usually associated with powerful men, yet she demonstrated courage and skill by taking part herself.”

Part 4

Nur Jahan’s actions in 1626

A political crisis

Why was this astonishing?

  • Leadership: she took control in a serious emergency.
  • Courage: she was prepared to risk her own safety.
  • Military involvement: she got involved in a situation normally controlled by men.
  • Determination: she fought to protect her husband and her influence.

Did it work?

  • Her attempt to defeat Mahabat Khan did not immediately succeed.
  • But her actions showed how powerful and determined she was.
  • She did not simply wait for male soldiers to solve the problem.
Exam sentence to remember“Nur Jahan’s actions in 1626 were astonishing because she took an active role in trying to rescue the emperor during a political crisis, demonstrating leadership and courage in a male-dominated world.”

Part 5

How did the Civil War impact women’s lives?

English Civil War, from 1642

1. New responsibilities

  • Many men left home to fight.
  • Women managed farms and businesses, and looked after children and families.
  • Some managed large estates while their husbands were away.

2. Violence and hardship

  • Armies sometimes took food and supplies.
  • Homes and farms were damaged, and families separated.
  • Some women became widows. Poverty and food shortages hit families.

3. More politically active

  • Some women signed and organised petitions, and protested against Parliament.
  • They supported either side, shared political and religious ideas, and gathered information.
  • Women could not vote or become MPs, but the war gave some a voice.

4. Involved in fighting

  • A small number helped defend towns and castles.
  • Some disguised themselves as men to fight.
  • Others nursed injured soldiers, or cooked, washed and carried supplies for armies.

Overall impact on women

Negative impactsPositive changes
Families were separatedMore responsibility
Some became widowsManaged farms and businesses
Homes were damagedBecame politically active
Food shortages and povertySome challenged traditional expectations

Remember: The Civil War did not suddenly make women equal to men. But it gave some women chances to become more independent and politically active.
Exam sentence to remember“The Civil War affected women’s lives because they faced hardship and violence, but some also gained greater responsibility by managing businesses, farms and households while men were away fighting.”

Part 6

How did the Civil War radicalise people?

Radicalise: to develop ideas that challenge the existing system

New questions people asked

  • Should the king have all the power?
  • Should ordinary people have a say in government?
  • Should everyone have religious freedom?
  • Should rich people own most of the land?

1. The king had been challenged

  • Many believed kings were chosen by God and must be obeyed.
  • But Parliament fought Charles I, and in 1649 he was put on trial and executed by his own people.

2. Soldiers had new ideas: the Putney Debates, 1647

  • Soldiers and thinkers in Parliament’s army discussed how England should be governed.
  • Who should vote? Should ordinary people have more rights? Should Parliament have more power?

5. Religious ideas changed

  • People questioned the authority of the Church.
  • Different Protestant groups wanted to worship in different ways, with fewer restrictions.
Exam sentence to remember“The Civil War radicalised people because the conflict weakened traditional authority and encouraged ordinary soldiers and citizens to question the power of the king and demand greater political and religious rights.”

Part 7

How did the Interregnum impact Britain?

1649 to 1660: “between reigns”

What was the Interregnum?

  • The time between the execution of Charles I in 1649 and the return of the monarchy in 1660.
  • England had no king, and the House of Lords was abolished.
  • England became a republic called the Commonwealth.

Oliver Cromwell

  • A key Parliamentarian leader of the New Model Army.
  • He helped defeat the Royalists and supported Charles I’s execution.
  • He became Lord Protector in 1653 and ruled until he died in 1658.
  • He often struggled to agree with Parliament, and used the army to keep control.

Stricter Puritan rules

  • Theatres were closed.
  • Bear-baiting was banned, and some sports were restricted.
  • Christmas celebrations outside church worship were banned.
  • Not everyone agreed with these rules.

Religion, Scotland and Ireland

  • Some Protestant groups got more freedom, and bishops were abolished.
  • Catholics were persecuted.
  • Military campaigns brought Scotland and Ireland under the Commonwealth, causing enormous suffering, especially in Ireland.

Overall impact of the Interregnum

Exam sentence to remember“The Interregnum changed Britain because the monarchy was abolished and England became a republic, but Cromwell’s rule also brought strict Puritan restrictions and political instability.”

Part 8

Why did the English want the king back?

The Restoration, 1660

After Cromwell died

  • Oliver Cromwell died in 1658, and his son Richard became Lord Protector.
  • Richard was inexperienced, and struggled to control the army or work with Parliament.
  • He resigned in 1659, and people looked for a different government.

Five reasons people wanted the king back

1

Political instability

  • Parliament and the army disagreed.
  • No strong leader could unite everyone.
2

End military rule

  • The army had become extremely powerful.
  • People wanted a government not run by soldiers.
3

Dislike of Puritan rules

  • Theatres were closed and Christmas restricted.
  • People wanted their traditional activities back.
4

Traditional government

  • Many preferred a king, a House of Lords and a House of Commons.
  • They thought a monarchy would bring order.
5

Charles II’s promises

  • The Declaration of Breda (1660) promised pardons, fair property settlements, religious leniency and soldiers’ pay.

The Restoration, 1660

  • The monarchy returned and Charles II became king.
  • The House of Lords returned, and traditional royal government was restored.
  • Theatres reopened, and Christmas could be celebrated openly again.
Exam sentence to remember“The English wanted the king back because they were unhappy with political instability, military rule and strict Puritan restrictions. Charles II’s promises in the Declaration of Breda also encouraged people to support the Restoration.”

A model 8-mark answer

Question: Why was Nur Jahan astonishing?

Nur Jahan was astonishing because she had an extraordinary amount of political power in the Mughal Empire.

Firstly, she influenced government decisions and issued royal orders. This was unusual because women were not normally expected to have this level of political authority.

Secondly, coins were produced bearing her name alongside Emperor Jahangir’s. This was astonishing because coins were an important symbol of royal power, showing that she was much more than just the emperor’s wife.

Finally, Nur Jahan was famous for her shikar, where she hunted dangerous animals such as tigers. This was usually an activity associated with powerful men, but she demonstrated courage and skill.

Overall, Nur Jahan was astonishing because she challenged traditional expectations of women and demonstrated political authority, courage and independence.

Remember: Exam tip: astonishing means very surprising. Always explain WHY each thing she did was unusual for a woman of her time.
